## What you get

Grace Ashcroft is the new blood: FBI analyst, tense, underpowered, actual resource management and stalking threats in first-person. It feels like RE7 and Village remembered why people liked being scared instead of just shooting pretty monsters.

Then Leon shows up and the game flips. Third-person, better guns, better movement, the classic “I’m too old for this shit but still better at it than you” energy. The switch isn’t seamless every time, but it’s honest about what each character is good for instead of forcing one system on both.

Raccoon City comes back looking like a place that actually got nuked and left to rot. Atmosphere holds. Combat in Leon sections has weight. Grace sections still make you check corners and count bullets. The story ties the old lore without turning into a six-hour lore dump.

## The annoying shit

Eleven hours is a bit short for seventy bucks. Some of the mid-game transitions feel like the game is clearing its throat before the next set piece. A couple of boss fights lean on spectacle more than clever design. And yes, there’s the usual Capcom “buy the deluxe if you want the extra costumes” nonsense at launch.

None of it breaks the game. It just reminds you they’re still Capcom.

## The verdict

They mixed the two Resident Evils people actually like, kept it tight, and didn’t drown it in corporate checklist shit. For once the marketing line about “best of both worlds” isn’t complete marketing wank.

Nice work. Don’t fuck up the next one.
